# Single-to-paired kettlebell and complex integration

Reviewed in this batch: #152 (double clean/press), #186 (double clean/front
squat), #366 (worked strength math); reused #365/#305/#335 from earlier reviews.

#152 explicitly directs learners to the double clean before combining it with
the press. #186 describes a broader single-to-double teaching sequence and
component practice before combinations. Those lessons do not prescribe the
app's two-exposure admission rule, 14-day evidence window, or a universal training
age. The source's multi-year/large-repetition examples remain contextual; they
are not automatic eligibility criteria. Double swing/outside-swing pathways are
not separately implemented yet.

App changes: recent single clean before paired clean; paired clean and single
press before double clean/press; distinct single-rack squat and paired clean
before double squat. Existing explicit technique and matched-pair requirements
remain. Supporting practice is scheduled when evidence is stale. These are
conservative application evidence gates informed by the teaching, not a claim
that every trainee must follow this exact source program.

Complexes keep their independent records. One completed sequence is one complex
rep; it does not also earn component exposures. The double triad explicitly
means clean → squat → press from rack, without an extra clean before the press.
The `doublePress` registry entry supplies the press skill context in that sequence,
not an instruction to repeat its full clean/press pair. Planning uses summed
component estimates with the existing conservative time floor; these seconds
are app estimates. Ordinary work remains untimed; EMOM/timed profiles retain
their actual structures. Prescriptions snapshot concise sequence explanations.

Saved history, old policy keys, active draft prescriptions and storage namespace
remain intact. The changed gates constrain future selection. Existing component
practice and deliberate skill confirmation remain separate from familiarity.
