# Training-rule implementation map

The authoritative prescriptions are `engine.js` and each movement's
`policyFor` provenance. Source facts, contextual examples and app decisions are
separate in `research/*evidence.json`. This document does not establish new source
support or certify technique.

## Evidence and advancement

- Actual results remain separate from the written prescription.
- Missing, skipped, unequal-sided, painful or technically failed work cannot earn
  advancement. Reduced return/recovery/time-fit work has no full-dose credit.
- Different movements and implements have independent cycles. Complex records
  do not award component progression. Explicit skill confirmation is separate
  from repetitions.
- Loading uses configured owned equipment; paired movements require matched
  inventory and record load per bell/club. Starting loads and later increases
  require explicit review. Source percentage examples are not universal conversions.
- Historical corrections replay progression and eligibility; old prescriptions
  and correction/undo history are retained. Timers never prove physical completion.

## Program composition: app decisions

Coordinated blocks retain their own source methods. Selection, spacing, workload
caps, recency windows and follow-up priority are configurable Strength Lab rules,
not a complete implementation of #548's full system. No endurance activity is
converted into fictional strength volume.

Zero-load starting-weight placeholders are setup needs, not executable work or
unfinished training debt. Coordinated plans omit them while retaining setup
reasons. Genuine partial/skipped prescribed work still carries forward. Existing
single-block setup behavior and historical records remain compatible.

Recent qualified descendant practice can maintain prerequisite recency only along
actual registry prerequisite edges. Every prerequisite must already have its own
clean evidence at the current policy/load, required skills and no unresolved pain
or newer failed work. The recent descendant also needs actual qualifying practice
and explicit required skills. Multi-level maintenance does not add foundation
repetitions, progression, load, mastery or future workout completion. Unrelated
movements and educational-only links cannot maintain eligibility. Corrections,
load/policy changes, failure and absence can require requalification.

Oversized rotations may still exceed a sustainable cadence. Warnings and bounded
follow-ups do not manufacture eligibility or add catch-up work.

## Persistence and delivery

Keep V33 storage keys, writer ownership, atomic state/draft transactions, backup
validation and correction replay intact. Eight Books stay separate from the
Movement Library. The preview is distinct from production; passing automation
is not physical Android or human technique verification.

## Hand-change practice

Source #185 supplies alternating hand-change instruction and two-hand swing
review, but no numerical program or compulsory breath cadence. The swingSkill
profile uses app-selected 3–5 reps per driving hand, 2–4 default sets, 90-second
rest, owned single-bell load review and explicit skill/recent-practice gates.
Five per hand means ten alternating swings total. Unequal/missing side counts
never earn completion credit. This cycle does not advance two-hand swings or
create automatic eligibility for paired kettlebell movements.


## Introductory single-arm swing

Source #67 teaches thumb-down/back, small swings, low introductory arm path,
inhale down/exhale up and controlled parking. #446 distinguishes hard-style
and soft-style endurance as separate skills. This new opt-in path covers the
introduction only. Confirm familiarity explicitly and retain recent qualified
two-hand practice. Complete one side, park, then repeat on the other; actual
bilateral counts are required. Sets, 3–5 reps per side, rest, planning pace,
evidence gates and load limits are Strength Lab app defaults. Existing
hand-change/paired gates and histories do not acquire a new prerequisite or
component progression. No source numerical cycle or technique mastery is claimed.


## Dated physical evidence

Prescriptions and prerequisite checks use records through the requested training
date (today when omitted). Later-dated imports/corrections remain in Training Log
but do not add current credit, failure counts, wave turns or supporting practice,
or erase an existing absence. Known unresolved pain is still held for review.
Date correction/undo replays the same original prescriptions and actuals.

Dated load review cannot qualify from later work. A valid later load-reset anchor
is retained and does not apply its pending increase before the anchor date;
a genuinely missing anchor still blocks replay. Explicit current settings and
unanchored starting loads stay current—no historical settings are invented.
Unscoped engine record/state inspection still exposes the full retained archive;
the current interface and dated actions request today's evidence explicitly.

Future unfinished work cannot replace real earlier debt. Existing explicit/legacy
pending work is preserved unless it is introduced solely by future unfinished
records; earlier physical debt is reconstructed from earlier written plans and
actuals. Forecasts freeze evidence at their starting date, even when displaying
later opportunities. Empty internal ordering placeholders can turn a provisional
slot but cannot earn progression, recency, mastery or load advancement. These
are application data/selection rules, not new transcript prescriptions.


## Optional row 60% / 80% volume wave

Source #89 gives an optional baseline-fraction example: 60% and 80% rep targets,
80% work at two-thirds of the 60% volume, add sets, and optional review after
4–6 weeks. Its example has two days of each type weekly. Strength Lab adapts
this to rolling opportunities; it does not prescribe a four-day week or force a
maximum/retest. The default fixed-dose row profile and its policy keys are unchanged.

The optional rowWave profile takes a user-reviewed clean baseline at a fixed bar
height. Rep fractions and higher-rep sets are floored to whole numbers; the
higher-rep target never exceeds two-thirds of the paired lower-rep total reps.
If one whole higher-rep set will not fit, an explicit too-small lower-rep starting
set count is rejected with the required minimum. Defaults are 2–4 lower-rep sets
(3 minimum where needed), 90-second rests and two qualifying exposures on each
track before jointly moving to the next lower-set stage. Higher-rep whole sets
may remain constant across a stage because of rounding. These numeric set ranges,
rests, joint gates and rounding are app choices, not a fully reproduced source week.

Only qualifying work alternates the progression tracks. Failed, incomplete,
changed-setup or recovery work cannot advance; recovery returns to the lower-rep
track and clears paired evidence. Setup changes do not count as technique failures.
Both tracks retain the shared pain, absence, owned-equipment and time-fitting
rules. Rows remain bodyweight and ordinary reps remain untimed. Higher-rep work
is not called a heavier external load and grants no pull-up readiness certificate.

Bar-height changes require explicit baseline review before this wave resumes.
Current actual geometry must still match its immutable prescription to qualify.
Baseline changes, same-number restarts and re-entering the profile get a separate
comparison identity, preventing reuse of an obsolete cycle. Profile settings and
history remain exportable; corrections and undo replay the same original targets.
At the endpoint, maintenance can alternate without increasing targets, or the user
can explicitly review/restart. Source frequency/retest timing, straight legs,
elevated feet, actual grip/angle measurement and physical technique validation
remain outside the automatic method.


## Shared Settings and Train controls

The persistent workout target is shared by all three duration selectors. Both
surfaces feed the same time budget and actual generator; a short target never
silently earns full-dose progression. The user explicitly replaced temporary
Train-only duration overrides with this shared preference.

Readiness is a dated choice, shared between Settings and Train. Low readiness
reduces actual planned work without ordinary progression credit; Great retains
normal progression rules. Same-day reload retains the choice; future days revert
to Normal. The unadjusted original and actual adjusted draft targets remain
separate. A timer cannot supply readiness, familiarity or completed sets.

Equipment availability and scheduling priority are distinct. Effective emphasis
shares and generator weights exclude families disabled by the equipment inclusion
flags while retaining their saved priority and owned weights. Emphasis balances
recorded physical practice in currently enabled, non-Off families. Older actuals
remain part of this accounting; a proposed shorter window is deferred because it
worsened existing multi-year continuity checks. This accounting never erases
history or resets movement-level progression. Deferred work and the four-skipped-
session fairness rule retain precedence. Shares are relative priorities, not exact
delivery percentages.

A style edit does not silently choose a different source program;
A/B/C variety/conditioning options do not rewrite independent source profiles.

Workout pacing is saved for new drafts. The active workout retains its own mode
and immutable written targets. Prescribed pacing uses the existing source-aware
timer queue; ordinary repetitions remain untimed. The separate custom interval
fields are now shared saved preferences. Editing resets custom playback, and
starting either timer pauses the other. Both remain independent of actual work
confirmation and advancement. These are application/control rules, not source
prescriptions or a change to exercise qualification.


## Optional paired-swing rep building

The default paired hinge method stays at fixed reps and adds sets. An explicitly
selected pairedSwingReps profile for doubleSwing uses two fixed sets at 6, 8, 10,
12, 14, 16, 18 and 20 reps per set. Source #142 discusses starting around six
and building toward twenty; fixed set count, intermediate steps, 90-second rest,
four-second-per-rep planning pace and clean-exposure qualification are app choices.
It is not a complete source-authored paired program.

Actual clean complete exposures advance the rep stage at the current owned
per-bell load. Incomplete, changed-load, changed-rest, painful, technically failed
or reduced work cannot advance it. Existing absence/recovery, experience/skill,
recent paired-deadlift/single-swing and matched-equipment checks remain mandatory.
Reps are untimed; timer activity never records work.

At twenty reps the app maintains rather than automatically increasing load.
A permitted owned increment requires explicit review and resets to six reps.
The optional profile has its own comparison identity. Returning from another
profile, or changing then restoring qualification/stall settings, starts a fresh
revision. Saving unchanged settings preserves progress. All old records, original
profile targets and current active-draft prescriptions remain intact.


## Completed endpoints and progression review

Completing a cycle and authorizing a new load/density decision are distinct.
A completed endpoint remains earned during absence, reduced work and maintenance.
Current dated review must pass the same readiness, re-entry, pain/skill and owned
load checks as an unreduced endpoint prescription. The latest movement attempt
must match the current policy, load, track, target and rest and be clean/full.
Reduced, incomplete, changed-target and changed-method work cannot certify a new
load or density action. Matching performed clean planned-light work is neutral.
One clean full endpoint maintenance workout restores review eligibility; this
does not invent another cycle or require repeating all earlier stages. These
are app reliability rules, not newly sourced numerical prescriptions.

Clean full maintenance clears an old failure count and updates the eligible
anchor. Density selection from this later anchor must replay correctly; correcting
that actual work can withdraw the transition and undo can restore it. Reduced
work with no actual sets cannot clear failures in either generic or row-wave
replay. Actual performed reduced work may still clear difficulty while remaining
nonqualifying for advancement.

Every live load/density action passes today's date. Undated helpers retain their
existing full-history audit behavior for compatibility with historical tools;
records/state audit reads remain unscoped unless a cutoff is supplied. Current
review never removes future records or rewrites earlier prescriptions.


## Prerequisite comparison evidence

Qualification uses the same dated load-reset boundary as progression. Before a
future reset event, the earlier comparison remains available; on/after it, older
records cannot supply new qualification. A genuinely missing anchor stays blocked
until explicit history repair. No history is removed.

Within that boundary, qualifying proof comes from the current uninterrupted
method/load comparison. A recorded attempt at another method/load separates
comparisons; returning to an older method or load cannot revive its old supporting
exposure count. Recency comes from actual comparable practice, rather than any
recent record for that movement. Configured wave light/heavy tracks are one
method; their current-load/track counts remain distinct. Matching clean planned
light work stays neutral, while reduced/incomplete practice remains nonqualifying.

Descendant maintenance applies these same rules to the descendant. A load reset
or changed comparison cannot let older child proof maintain stale ancestors.
Fresh qualifying practice can restore the pathway; maintenance never adds parent
exposures or progression. These are app evidence-consistency rules, not new
transcript numerical requirements or automatic technique certification.


## Configured heavy/light wave replay

The existing wave method keeps its configured light/heavy loads, light progression
stages and derived heavy whole-set targets. Actual load mistakes never replace
those configured loads. Records stay intact, including failed and wrong-load work.
Only successful complete work at the expected track, configured load and current
target/rest turns the wave. Light work advances the existing light stages; heavy
work clears heavy difficulty against its own derived target. It does not invent
independent heavy stages or extra completed cycles.

Source #228 supports lower heavy total work and whole-repetition/set arithmetic;
#403 supplies long-term heavy/light context. Existing rep choices, alternation,
qualification counts and recovery settings are app decisions. Heavy work stays
strictly below the last qualifying light exposure, including after stall
redistribution or other reductions. Whole-set bounds are checked against the
computed work; impossible or nonfinite arithmetic cannot produce a heavy set.
No automatic pair increase or universal source percentage is added.

Reduced or failed work does not advance the turn. Correct performed reduced work
may clear difficulty; zero-set recovery cannot. Corrections/undo and date cutoffs
replay the same rules without rewriting original prescriptions. Shared wave stage
reporting follows light progression, while current-track failures stay separate.


## Optional planned lighter exposures

The existing Settings interval (zero disables it) counts full qualifying normal
work for each movement since its last actually delivered planned deload. It applies
to volume, density and earned-endpoint maintenance. It is an app preference, not
a source-mandated universal schedule. Current method/written-load comparisons,
explicit load resets and evidence dates bound the count. Incorrect actual loads
cannot erase owed work at an unchanged written load.

A clean completed lighter target services the interval without ordinary advancement
credit. Later light, recovery, re-entry, rebuild, time-fit, prerequisite or partial
work cannot retrigger that same interval or count as normal qualifying work. An
incomplete, painful, wrong-load, rest-invalid or setup-changed deload leaves it
due. Structured notebook records are re-evaluated from actual sets; valid older
aggregate evidence remains readable. Corrections/undo and future cutoffs use the
same replay without changing original prescriptions.

Lighter work reduces whole sets, or reps/time for a single-set prescription. Where
no smaller positive whole-unit dose exists, explain the minimum practice rather
than imply a reduction. It still grants no advancement credit. Normal endpoint
review and heavy-wave work caps remain in force.


## Recovery and return work fractions

Low-readiness and re-entry fractions are app recovery choices, not new transcript
programs. They reduce whole sets; when only one set is prescribed, they reduce
whole reps or seconds instead. Low-readiness upward rounding cannot retain every
normal set when the selected fraction is below one. Existing multi-set half-dose
rounding and get-up total-time 30-second rounding remain.

Combined low readiness and return/review never increase either already reduced
quantity. Keep familiar loads, rests, timing and bilateral requirements. Reduced
work receives no ordinary advancement credit even when its own target is fully
performed. Actuals and written targets remain independently recorded.

A one-rep minimum or 30-second total-time minimum may be irreducible; explain this
instead of claiming less work. Imported fraction 1 remains allowed and explicitly
means configured full-dose non-advancing practice. Inactive baselines and blocked
zero-set wave prescriptions never become valid through recovery rounding. Heavy
work remains below its light-work cap. No policy identity or history migration.


## Load setup before loaded recovery

Recovery, absence and reviewed-return adaptations do not replace an unset working
load. Unstarted loaded movements remain setup/baseline targets in a single-block
plan. Coordinated plans omit executable zero-load targets and may choose eligible
unloaded preparation. Genuine prerequisites and unresolved pain keep their own
blocks. Only an explicit owned initial-load selection activates loaded recovery;
no load is selected automatically, and established-load guards remain.

A historical zero-load placeholder with no actual sets is setup-only even when an
older version labelled it recovery, re-entry, rebuild, deload or time-fit. It does
not become owed physical work or retain unfinished-block priority. Evaluate this
from dated records without changing saved history/targets. Positive actual work,
real partial loaded work, zero-load bodyweight work and unrelated explicit/legacy
pending work retain their meaning. Future placeholders cannot clear today's debt.
Pain review is retained independently; setup cannot dismiss a pain hold.

Require explicit zero-load evidence for inferred historical setup-only work.
Missing older load metadata is unknown and must retain its existing pending-work
meaning; never infer zero from an absent field.


## Reviewed-baseline cycle comparisons

For push-up and row 60%/80% methods, changing a cycle-defining setting starts a
new comparison revision. This includes baseline, fraction/step where supported,
exposure requirement, stall choice and row set range/reviewed baseline height.
Changing then restoring a setting cannot revive an earlier cycle's credit, even
if two reviewed baselines round to the same reps. Unchanged effective settings
preserve the current key and credit; no history migration is required.

Explicit baseline review/restart creates exactly one new revision, including a
same-number review. A changed-number review does not increment twice. Revisions
are safe nonnegative integers; a caller cannot lower them or silently overflow.
Old physical work and written targets stay visible. Fresh actual work, correction
and undo drive only the new comparison. Fixed-dose row history stays independent.

Full #609 and #89 source text was checked for this repair. Existing EMOM span,
baseline fractions, row volume cap, rests, exposure gates and rolling timing stay
unchanged. No required maximum test or automatic retest schedule is introduced.


## Rolling training-day capacity in forecasts

Actual planning and provisional opportunities share the existing weekly capacity:
requested days, the saved maximum training days and the existing four-day strength
style ceiling. These are app scheduling choices. No new source frequency or
universal rest rule is inferred. The calendar also retains its three-day default
when no requested frequency exists. A lower maximum never rewrites the request.

Count distinct physical dates and proposed opportunity dates within each rolling
seven-day window. Partial physical work spends a training day; duplicate sessions
on that date do not spend extra days. A forecast remains provisional, never
creates physical evidence or progression, and cannot use future actuals early.
Changing a limit moves opportunities rather than erasing past work. Manual moves
remain lower bounds under recovery/capacity rules and do not consume the queue.

Outside activity, pauses, specific demand spacing and active draft immutability
still apply. The retention fallback uses configured capacity before sufficient
observed cadence exists; it cannot assume seven feasible days under a lower cap.
Observed cadence remains feedback from actual work rather than invented dates.


## Delivered reduced targets and return acknowledgement

Rebuild, re-entry, recovery and deload records acknowledge their purpose only
when the full written reduced target is cleanly performed. Positive actual sets
alone do not clear accumulated difficulty. Structured notebook records are
re-evaluated against their saved prescription and actual sets: quantity, load,
setup, bilateral work, cadence and continuity must agree. Valid older aggregate
quantities remain readable without invented sets; unknown targets fail closed.

Generic, row-wave and configured-wave replay share this delivery check. Existing
wave track matching and heavy-work caps remain. A genuine generic actual-load
change still follows its existing fresh-comparison rules; these are independent
of recovery acknowledgement. Reduced work grants no ordinary cycle advancement.

The user's explicit pain-review decision remains separate from the subsequent
required return work. Only a performed, clean re-entry/recovery matching its own
written target clears that return requirement. Wrong actual load or changed
station geometry cannot certify it. This is app evidence consistency, not medical
clearance or a new source prescription. Pain holds, review keys and written/actual
history stay intact; correction/undo and date cutoffs recalculate acknowledgement.


## Unfinished physical returns

Positive actual sets establish physical attendance; they do not by themselves
finish a prescribed re-entry target. A failed/incomplete or mismatched re-entry
creates a history-derived pending return. Normal or great readiness does not
bypass it. Subsequent plans remain reduced until clean reduced work is delivered.
A genuine later successful ordinary record already in older history also settles
the physical return without adding new credit or rewriting that history.

The separate reviewed-pain requirement still needs its existing performed clean
re-entry/recovery evidence. Ordinary history cannot bypass that requirement.
Old zero-load/zero-set setup placeholders do not create physical return debt,
and active regular difficulty is not relabelled as absence. Working-load/method
comparison and advancement rules remain independent. No new source dose is added.

Pending status is derived at the evidence date. Future delivery cannot clear a
current return; correcting dates/actuals or undoing a correction recalculates it.
Original prescriptions and active drafts remain snapshots. Movement status and
next-dose explanations reflect the unfinished return consistently.


## Light practice at a changed station

Clean performed light work preserves a progression streak only at the written
station setup. A changed bar height/leg position or step height/pattern interrupts
the streak, matching the existing prerequisite-evidence rule. These clean geometry
edits do not create technique failures or erase an earned stage. The actual
physical work and original targets remain visible; history corrections/undo replay
the comparison. Matching-station light practice stays neutral and never adds an
ordinary advancement exposure. This is comparison reliability, not a new exercise
method or numerical prescription.
